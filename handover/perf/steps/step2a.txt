Then, each in its own commit with a test (integration against real git processes and repositories; scenes for app behaviour; unit tests for pure functions), measured before and after:
1. Git.run (Git.swift ~27) has no cancellation handler: a cancelled task leaves git running and the awaiting scope blocked until it exits, and stdout is buffered without a limit. It also parks 2-3 threads per process. Make a cancelled run terminate its process (and its group) promptly, bound what it buffers (callers say how much they can use; over the bound is an error the caller handles, not a silent truncation), and stop parking threads (dispatch sources or async reads). Steps 4-5 rely on this for superseding refreshes.
2. ObjectReader (the cat-file --batch reader) lives for the app's lifetime, keyed per root, and copies through [UInt8] (3x per blob, per git.md). Give it an owner and a lifetime (closed with its repository or when idle), share one per object store rather than per root, and read blobs without extra copies. Check git.md's fd-leak-via-dup lead.
3. prepareNeighbors starts uncancelled whole-file work for i+1, i-1, i+2 on every step, including pathological neighbours (audit 2's 7.7 GB peak). Bound it (one neighbour ahead in the reading direction, a byte budget) and cancel what the next step makes obsolete, unless another consumer still needs the result.
4. Pane and session lifetimes: 1c stops a window's work when it closes; check what's left when a pane closes or a session is dropped while the window stays (Session.indexTask keeps indexing after its session is gone; a closed pane's Reader.analysis and pending loads retain the Reader; plan.md "chrome" section) and fix it.
5. Caches: Session.explored is never evicted, and other caches (blob contents, lines, analyses) have no bound. Introduce one byte-bounded, O(1) LRU (by bytes actually held, not entries) that these share, sized by a single budget; evict what nothing shows. Measure resident memory in the soak and linux scenarios before and after.
